## Round 2 — The Attack (Aggressive Critique & Veto Enforcement)

**HARVEY SPECTER:** Hold it right there on `Candidates #1, #6, #7, and #8`! The moment you touch cross-border stablecoins (`PYUSD`) and India/EU/US corridors, regulators draw their guns [S16]. In the US, **FinCEN Travel Rule** and **OFAC sanctions screening** apply; in New York, **NYDFS** regulates Paxos/PYUSD; in the EU, **MiCA** applies; and in India, the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI) FEMA Export of Goods and Services Regulations** and **Income Tax Act VDA rules (30% tax + 1% TDS under Section 194S)** severely penalize holding or trading unhedged crypto on Indian corporate balance sheets [S4, S16]! **I am placing a standing Legal Veto on any cross-border PYUSD architecture that forces an Indian or restricted-jurisdiction exporter to take custody of raw VDA tokens in violation of FEMA/tax rules!**

**DR. R.S. SHARMA:** Harvey is 100% right on Indian law [S16]. How do we structure `Candidate #1` (`TreasuryFlow PYUSD`) and `Candidate #7` (`GlobalPod Payroll`) so Harvey's and my veto is lifted? We use **Asymmetric Dual-Rail Settlement**:
1. For **US/EU/SG B2B counter-parties** where PYUSD is a regulated 1:1 USD instrument (issued by Paxos under NYDFS), they can settle invoices via **Solana Token-2022 Confidential Transfers + Memo Required**, and the agent calls `record_payment_for_invoice` on PayPal Invoicing v2 [S2, S4, S5].
2. For **Indian exporters and restricted corridors**, the US buyer can still pay via PayPal Orders v2 ("Pay with Crypto" / PYUSD or `PAY_BY_BANK` / Fastlane), while PayPal settles **100% compliant fiat INR** to the Indian exporter's bank account with automatic **FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate)** and GST LUT invoice metadata attached via `create_invoice` [S2, S4, S16]! Zero VDA tax trap, 100% FEMA compliant!

**CHARLIE MUNGER:** Good legal rescue. Now here is my operational pre-mortem on `Candidate #1`: what happens if a buyer sends 500 PYUSD on Solana with a typo in the memo field, or sends 480 PYUSD on a $500 invoice, and your agent blindly marks the entire $500 PayPal invoice paid via `record_payment_for_invoice` [S2, S5]? **Veto unless the reconciliation engine enforces exact cryptographic invoice-hash matching in the Token-2022 `Memo Required` field and handles partial payments to the cent!**

**ANDREJ KARPATHY:** Easy deterministic fix: when our agent creates the PayPal Invoice (`create_invoice`), it embeds a deterministic payment URI containing `invoice_id` and `exact_amount_atomic_units`. On Solana Devnet/Mainnet, the Token-2022 `MemoTransfer` extension rejects any transfer lacking a memo, and our reconciliation verifier checks `amount_received == invoice_balance` (or logs a partial payment in `record_payment_for_invoice`) before updating state [S2, S5].

**ROELOF BOTHA & WARREN BUFFETT:** Let's check the unit economics [S3, S4]. On a $25,000 B2B invoice, traditional cross-border wire + FX costs ~3% to 6% ($750 to $1,500) and takes 4 days [S3]. Settling via PayPal Invoicing v2 + PYUSD/Pay-by-Bank with a dynamic conditional rule (`create_conditional_rules_for_invoice`) cuts settlement time to 2 seconds and saves $600+ per invoice—giving us massive B2B SaaS pricing power ($199–$499/month per treasury workspace) [S2, S3, S4].
